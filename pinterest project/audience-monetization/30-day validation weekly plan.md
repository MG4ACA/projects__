# FutureNest 30-Day Validation

## Weekly Plan for Approval

**Start date:** 2026-08-25  
**Duration:** 4 weeks  
**Time budget:** Approximately 10 hours per week  
**Working URL:** `future.lumicore-labs.com`  
**Hosting:** Hostinger KVM1 VPS  
**Primary audience:** Wild Build Pinterest audience, starting with Future Living / Off-grid Tech

## Objective

Test whether the Wild Build Pinterest audience can produce measurable, commercially useful traffic for a small FutureNest-style website.

The experiment must answer four questions:

1. Can Pinterest users be persuaded to visit the website?
2. Will those visitors read the content and click product links?
3. Can we obtain at least one affiliate program that accepts the publisher and supports practical payouts from Sri Lanka?
4. Is the resulting traffic and intent strong enough to justify continuing?

This is a validation experiment, not a full product launch. We will not build an AI planner, login system, marketplace, mobile app, or second niche site during these 30 days.

## Working Hypotheses

- **H1:** Future Living / Off-grid content will generate the most qualified traffic.
- **H2:** Specific, useful Pin promises will produce more outbound clicks than generic inspiration Pins.
- **H3:** Product comparison and buying-guide pages will produce measurable product-link clicks.
- **H4:** At least one affiliate network or merchant will offer a workable path after eligibility and payout checks.

## Success Criteria

The experiment is successful enough to continue if, by Day 30, we have:

- At least **100 Pinterest-attributed website sessions**.
- At least **5% of Pinterest-referred sessions clicking a product link**.
- At least one affiliate program approved or realistically obtainable.
- A working measurement path from Pin to article to product click.
- Enough data to identify one promising topic or product category.

These are decision thresholds, not revenue guarantees.

## Week 1: Foundation and Eligibility

**Goal:** Make the destination technically reachable and confirm whether monetization is possible.

### Priorities

### Deliverables

- Basic deployment/release process.
- Affiliate-program tracker with at least five candidates.
- Analytics and event-tracking checklist completed.

### Explicitly defer

- Branding polish and logo work.
- AI features.
- Full CMS or admin dashboard.
- Affiliate product claims that have not been verified.

## Week 2: Minimum Website and Content

### September 1-7

**Goal:** Publish a small, useful destination that can receive traffic.

### Build

- Homepage with a clear Future Living value proposition.
- One `/off-grid` or equivalent category page.
- Two commercial articles.
- One inspiration article.
- Reusable product card with merchant link status.
- Affiliate disclosure, privacy page, and contact page.
- Mobile layout, page metadata, sitemap, and robots file.
- Redirect or tracking endpoint for product clicks.

### Initial article topics

1. **10 Off-grid Gadgets You Can Actually Buy**
2. **7 Solar-powered Home Upgrades Worth Comparing**
3. **12 Tiny-home Technologies That Make Small Spaces Work**

Use the article brief template before writing. Every commercial article must explain selection criteria, limitations, and who each recommendation is for. Do not publish unverified prices, commission rates, or product availability.

### Deliverables

- Public MVP with three articles.
- At least 10 candidate products recorded in the affiliate tracker.
- Article briefs and source notes stored in `pinterest-post-generation/`.
- First analytics test completed with a test visit and test product click.

## Week 3: Pinterest Funnel Experiment

### September 8-14

**Goal:** Send a controlled batch of high-intent traffic to the MVP.

### Publishing plan

- Create 15-20 original Pins.
- Produce 3-5 Pin angles for each article.
- Use concrete promises such as comparisons, checklists, budgets, and use cases.
- Link each Pin to the matching article, never directly to an affiliate merchant for this test.
- Apply a consistent UTM campaign and record each Pin in the campaign tracker.
- Publish across the strongest relevant Wild Build board, starting with Future Living / Off-grid Tech.

### Measurement cadence

Record results at baseline, then at least twice during the week:

- Impressions.
- Engagements.
- Pin clicks.
- Outbound clicks.
- Pinterest-attributed sessions.
- Article engagement.
- Product-link clicks.
- Affiliate redirect clicks.

### Deliverables

- 15-20 Pins published or scheduled.
- Every Pin mapped to a landing page and campaign identifier.
- First performance snapshot completed.
- One short note identifying the strongest Pin promise and weakest Pin promise.

## Week 4: Analysis and Decision

### September 15-21

**Goal:** Decide whether to continue, reposition, or stop the experiment.

### Review

Compare the campaign tracker, analytics events, and affiliate tracker. Separate:

- Pinterest attention.
- Website traffic.
- Content consumption.
- Product intent.
- Actual affiliate availability.
- Revenue, if any.

### Decision rules

**Continue and expand** if traffic and product intent are measurable, an affiliate path is workable, and one topic performs better than the rest. Create five more articles in that topic and repeat the winning Pin pattern.

**Continue but reposition** if Pinterest sends visitors but product interest is weak. Improve comparison content, add a useful calculator or checklist, or test a service/product angle before expanding the catalog.

**Pause or stop** if several Pin angles produce no meaningful traffic, the website cannot be operated within 10 hours per week, or affiliate approval and payout constraints make the model impractical.

### Deliverables

- Completed validation results dashboard.
- One-page decision memo: continue, reposition, or stop.
- List of the top three lessons from the experiment.
- Recommended next 30-day scope, only if the result supports continuing.

## Operating Rules

- Measure before adding features.
- Keep the transcript and strategy notes separate from execution records.
- Use the latest CSV export for comparisons and record its date range.
- Treat impressions as reach, not proof of business value.
- Verify affiliate terms directly before relying on them.
- Do not publish medical, safety, energy-sizing, or financial claims without authoritative sources.
- Keep the experiment focused on one audience cluster and one destination.
